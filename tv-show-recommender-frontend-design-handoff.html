Concept & UX Philosophy

The Problem We Solve

When couples, families, or friend groups sit down to watch TV together, the "what should we watch?" argument can kill the mood entirely. Everyone has different tastes, and endlessly scrolling through Netflix while someone vetoes every suggestion is exhausting. PickTogether removes the friction with a single mechanic: each person gets an AI-personalised recommendation based on their taste profile, and when you want to watch together, you settle it with Rock Paper Scissors. Fast, fair, a little fun.

Core UX Principles

1. Seamless — zero decision fatigue. The app makes the recommendation for you. You never browse. You arrive, you see one show, you either watch it or challenge someone. Every screen has a single clear primary action.

2. Social without being exhausting. Friends are useful — you can see what they're watching, nudge them to watch the same thing, and settle conflicts by playing a quick RPS match. There are no feeds to scroll, no likes, no comments.

3. Punchy micro-moments. The RPS result reveal (3 → 2 → 1 countdown, winner flash) is the emotional centrepiece. It should feel like a mini game show. Keep it snappy — under 3 seconds total.

4. Content-first visual design. Show poster art dominates every screen. The UI chrome recedes; the show becomes the hero. Dark gradient overlays on poster images, large bold typography, generous negative space.

App Name & Identity

App name: PickTogether

Accent palette: Indigo → Purple gradient (#6366f1 → #a855f7). Used for CTAs, active states, brand moments. Never for destructive actions.

Background: Off-white #f8f7fc for main screens, pure white #ffffff for cards. Deep navy/purple for the splash and RPS result screens to signal "event mode".

Icon: TV screen with a play arrow — simple, scalable. Rounded-rect app icon with the indigo→purple gradient fill.

Screen-by-Screen Design Decisions

Splash / Auth

Deep navy-to-purple full-bleed gradient. Logo centred vertically at ~45% from top (feels higher than geometric centre — optical illusion). Tagline is two lines max: "Stop arguing. Start watching." / "One show everyone agrees on." Social login buttons stacked vertically — Google first (larger audience), Apple second. No email/password to eliminate friction.

On first launch after auth: route to Onboarding. On return launch: route directly to Home. Handle via checking tasteProfile existence before navigation.

Onboarding

One question per screen — never more. The progress bar at top shows exactly where the user is. Back navigation is always available. The "Continue" button is disabled until at least one option is selected (hard validation, not just a warning). Questions 1 and 8 are multi-select; all others are single-select.

Chip layout: Wrap flow, left-aligned, 8px gap. Selected chips use the brand gradient fill. The chip height is 36px (generous touch target). On single-select questions, selecting one chip auto-deselects any other.

Onboarding tone: Keep it light — "What genres do you love?" not "Select your preferred content categories." The copy should feel like a friend asking, not a form.

Home Screen

This is the app's heart. It must answer two questions instantly: "What should I watch tonight?" and "Want to sort out a group watch?" The recommendation card is the hero — poster art fills most of its height, with a gradient overlay and title/meta overlaid. Below it sits the "Watch Together" session widget, which is the social entry point.

Recommendation card poster height: 220px. The gradient overlay goes from transparent at 50% to 70% opacity black at the bottom. Show title in 20px/800 weight white. Genres and network as small pills.

Countdown label between card and session widget: small, muted, e.g. "Next pick in · 18h 22m". It should not compete visually with the card.

Empty state: If no recommendation exists yet, show an illustrated placeholder (e.g. a TV set with a "?" in screen) and copy: "Your first pick is on its way — we're personalising it now."

Onboarding → Home transition

After the last onboarding question, do not navigate immediately. Show a brief "Building your taste profile..." loading screen (1–2 seconds, animated progress bar or spinner) before routing to Home. This sets the expectation that something personalised is happening and makes the first recommendation feel earned.

RPS Flow — Key UX Decisions

Pick Show screen: Default-select today's recommendation with a highlighted border. The TVDB search option is secondary (collapsed or below the rec card). Most users will just use their daily rec.

Choose R/P/S screen: Both shows are displayed as side-by-side posters with "VS" in the middle — this is the moment of competitive framing. The three RPS buttons are large (80px height), icon + label, equal width. Once tapped, the entire picker disables and a pulsing "Waiting for [name]..." label appears. This screen must respond to Realtime — when both picks are in, auto-navigate without user action.

Result screen: Dark mode only. 3→2→1 countdown using large text (80px+), each step fades in/out over 600ms. Then the winner card slides up. The losing show is NOT shown — only the winner's show is featured, keeping focus on what to watch next, not on who lost.

Friends Screen

Each friend row shows their avatar, name, and what they're watching today (poster thumbnail + title). The "Nudge" button sends a push notification to the friend encouraging them to watch the same show. It disables after use (until next day) to prevent spam — label changes to "Sent ✓".

Pending requests banner: High-contrast blue banner at the very top when there are pending requests. Dismisses once requests are handled.

Map Screen

The map is a discovery and social proof surface — it shows users they're not alone in their tastes. Initial region = contiguous US. Each pin is a small card bubble (show poster thumbnail + title) anchored to a zip code centroid. Tapping a pin shows a callout with the show + "N people in this area".

Pin clustering: If many zips have the same show, consider a heat-map overlay instead of overlapping pins. For hackathon scope, individual pins are fine.

Rock Paper Scissors Screens

Real-time note: All RPS screens after a match is created subscribe to a Supabase Realtime channel on rps_matches for their match ID. UI updates reactively — no manual refresh needed.
RPS Home /rps

Shows active/pending matches. "New Challenge" button starts the flow. Incoming challenges appear as cards with Accept/Decline.

  • ActiveMatchCardShows opponent name, status badge, expires countdown if pending.
  • IncomingChallengeCardOpponent name, their show pick, Accept / Decline buttons.
Pick Show /rps/pick-show

Two options: use today's recommendation (pre-filled card shown) or search TVDB. TVDB search is a text input that hits the TVDB /search endpoint and returns a list of shows.

  • TodaysRecOptionTappable card showing today's rec. Selected state = highlighted border.
  • TVDBSearchInputSearch input with debounced query. Shows TVDBSearchResult[] list below.
  • TVDBSearchResultRow with poster thumbnail, show title, year, network. Tap to select.
Pick Opponent /rps/pick-opponent

List of accepted friends. Tap one to confirm and send the challenge. Shows selected show at top for context.

  • FriendPickerListScrollable friend list. Each row: avatar, name, tap to select.
  • SelectedShowHeaderSmall sticky header showing your chosen show so the user remembers what they picked.
Waiting for Acceptance /rps/waiting/[matchId]

Shown to the challenger while waiting for the opponent to accept. Counts down from 30 minutes. Cancel button revokes the challenge.

  • ExpiryCountdownMM:SS countdown. Pulls expires_at from match. Turns red under 5 min.
  • WaitingAnimationSimple pulsing indicator — no heavy animations.

When Realtime fires with status = "accepted", auto-navigate to Choose screen.

Choose R/P/S /rps/choose/[matchId]

Both users land here simultaneously. Shows both shows (yours vs. opponent's). Three large buttons: Rock, Paper, Scissors. Once you tap, your choice is locked and submitted. Shows "Waiting for opponent..." after you've chosen.

  • ShowVersusCardSide-by-side layout: your show poster vs. opponent's. "VS" divider in the middle.
  • RPSPickerThree large buttons with icons (✊ ✋ ✌️). Disabled after selection. Selected button shows filled/active state.
  • OpponentStatusSmall label: "Waiting for {name}..." or "Opponent is ready!"

When Realtime fires with both choices set, auto-navigate to Result screen.

Result /rps/result/[matchId]

Animated reveal screen. Shows 3-2-1 countdown, then flips to reveal winner and winning show. Both users see this simultaneously via Realtime.

  • CountdownRevealLarge centred "3 → 2 → 1" number sequence, then fades to result.
  • RPSResultCardWinner name + "WINS!" label, winning show poster + title, both RPS choices shown side by side.
  • WatchTogetherCTA"Add to watchlist" or "Share" button. Optional — nice to have.

Design Notes

Tone

Casual, social, fun. This is a recommendation + social app — it should feel closer to Letterboxd or Spotify than a utility app. Use show poster art liberally since TVDB provides it.

Typography & Spacing

Use the system font stack. Minimum 14px body, 16–20px for titles. Generous touch targets (min 44×44pt per Apple HIG). Bottom tab bar should account for safe area insets on both iOS and Android.

RPS Screens

These are the highest-energy screens — the Choose and Result screens in particular should feel reactive and punchy. The countdown (3-2-1) before the result reveal is a key moment of delight. Keep animations short and snappy, no looping.

Loading States

Every screen that fetches data needs a skeleton loader — not a spinner. The RecommendationCard skeleton should mimic the poster + text layout. TVDB search results should show 3–4 skeleton rows while fetching.

Empty States

Every list screen (Friends Feed, RPS Home, Promotions) needs a designed empty state — a short message and a relevant CTA. No blank white screens.
